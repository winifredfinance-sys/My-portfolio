How I work

A loop, not a line.

  1. Understand
  2. Define
  3. Prioritise
  4. Design
  5. Deliver
  6. Measure
  7. Improve
  8. Back to Understand

Measurement usually sends me back to discovery. How deep each stage goes depends on the product's maturity, the business context and what the team can carry.

Discovery and definition

Funnel data, user feedback, market research and workflow reviews, so the problem is clear before anyone sketches a solution.

Prioritisation

User feedback, business value and delivery constraints weighed together, with the trade-offs written down for stakeholders to see.

Delivery and learning

Agile delivery with regular releases, KPIs and OKRs, product analytics and A/B testing that decide what ships next.

Principles

Principles I hold

  1. Name the metric before the feature.

    Checkout conversion at Shopta, course completion at Edswot, fraud incidents at Trustesse. If I can't say which number a feature should move, it isn't ready for the backlog.

  2. Find the bottleneck before adding features.

    At Movam, demand was not the constraint. Drivers lost in a paper registration process were. So onboarding went first.

  3. Operations teams are users too.

    Internal teams carry the cost of bad workflows. Workflow reviews with them surface problems no customer survey will.

  4. Design for the least confident user.

    Some drivers weren't comfortable with technology, so we kept a manual approval path instead of full automation. Inclusion protected supply.

  5. Cut scope, not quality.

    At the health insurer, bulk upload of dependants mattered, but members could add their own. We deferred it and shipped the core portal properly.

AI in my product practice

AI drafts. People decide.

I use AI-assisted workflows to move faster through research synthesis, requirements, user stories, acceptance criteria, edge-case analysis and test planning. Judgment and ownership stay with me and the team.

  1. 1 · HumanFrame the problemWho has it, what it costs, which metric should move.
  2. 2 · AI-assistedDraft fastSynthesis, requirements, stories, edge cases, test plans.
  3. 3 · HumanReview togetherChecked with design, engineering and operations.
  4. 4 · HumanDecide and ownI make the call and own the outcome.
  5. 5 · TeamShip and measureResults feed the next loop.

Where I draw the line

AI output is a draft. I validate it against real users and real constraints before it reaches the backlog, and I don't let it make decisions that need context it doesn't have.

What I'd change from my own past work

Early at Movam, requirements, edge cases and test planning were slow and manual. I now bring AI in at the start of that work to shorten delivery, and keep human approval wherever judgment matters.

About

Commercial first, then product.

Before I worked in product, I ran my own business.

From 2019 to 2022 I founded and ran Maloxe, an apparel brand in Lagos selling clothing to wholesale and retail buyers. I sourced stock, set prices, managed customers and watched cash flow. That is where I learned to think commercially: every decision has a cost, a customer and a consequence.

I joined Movam Technologies in January 2023 as a Product Manager and was promoted to Senior Product Manager in November 2025. Alongside it I took contract and consulting product roles in e-commerce, fintech security and education. Today I'm Product Team Lead at a Nigerian health insurer, leading product managers, senior engineers and QA.

Across those roles I've delivered 30+ product features and improved team efficiency by 20% through workflow automation and sprint optimisation. I studied Biochemistry at Crawford University, which shaped how I treat evidence: I want to know what the data actually shows before I decide.
