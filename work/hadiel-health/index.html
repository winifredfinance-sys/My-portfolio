Self-service for corporate health plan clients

HR teams and agencies depended on Hadiel Health for every piece of information about their own staff. I lead the team of 20 that replaced that queue with a client portal, and cut agency onboarding from up to two days to under 10 minutes.

Context

Hadiel Health is a health maintenance organisation (HMO) in Lagos that provides health plans to companies. Client HR teams, and the agencies that manage staff for them, need to see who is covered, how the plan is being used and how the numbers reconcile. Members need to see their own plan.

The problem

For clients, every question about their own people went through the HMO: utilisation, member lists, reconciliation. They asked, then waited for answers.

For Hadiel Health, each request was manual work for the team. Onboarding a new agency was slow and manual too: the agency emailed a spreadsheet, our team uploaded it, and we sent the details back. That took up to two days.

My role and team

I am Product Team Lead for a cross-functional team of 20: three product managers, two senior back-end engineers, two senior front-end engineers, QA and others.

My job is direction and trade-offs: what the team builds, in what order, and what we deliberately leave out. The product managers on my team own their areas day to day; I make sure those areas add up to one product.

Discovery

I confirmed what clients needed in two ways:

  • Interviews with clients, about what they asked us for and why.
  • Feedback from the operations teams who serve them: enrolment officers, customer service, case officers, provider relations officers and agency relations officers. They handle the requests every day, so they knew which ones came up again and again.

Key insight

Clients did not need more reports from us. They needed to see their own data without asking.

Options and trade-offs

Bulk upload of dependants: deprioritised. It mattered, but members add their own dependants in the enrollee app, so it had less impact than other work, and time was tight. I chose to ship the core portal well instead of shipping more of it thinly.

Deactivations stay with Hadiel Health. Clients manage their members in the portal, but deactivations are approved on the admin side. Self-service where it saves time; control where a mistake would cost a member their cover.

What we built

  • Client portal for HR teams and agencies: upload and manage members, view utilisation, and see health-data summaries and reconciliation data, without contacting the HMO each time. Data syncs from the admin platform, and deactivations are approved on the admin side.
  • Enrollee app for members: their plan, benefits, providers and dependants.

Outcomes

2 days → under 10 minagency onboarding time, now done by the agency in the portal
20people in the cross-functional team I lead
2products: client portal and enrollee app

Clients now get the information they used to request from us directly in the portal, which removed a steady stream of back-and-forth for both sides.

What I'm measuring next

  • Client satisfaction (CSAT) after onboarding.
  • Volume of information requests from clients to operations.
  • Time from agency sign-up to first active member.
  • Weekly active clients on the portal.

My leadership practice

I lead a cross-functional team of 20: 3 PMs, 2 senior back-end engineers, 2 senior front-end engineers, QA and others. The rhythm I run:

  • Weekly performance updates, so progress and blockers are visible every week.
  • Daily stand-ups to keep the work moving.
  • Sprint planning, where priorities and trade-offs are agreed up front.
  • Sprint retrospectives, so the team improves how it works, not only what it ships.

With three product managers on the team, I keep priorities explicit and trade-offs written down, so engineering and QA are not resolving product questions in code.

What I learned and what's next

Leading PMs changes the job. My leverage is no longer in writing every requirement. It is in making the trade-offs clear enough that three PMs and their engineers make the same call I would.

Next: revisit bulk dependant upload when usage shows whether clients still need it.
