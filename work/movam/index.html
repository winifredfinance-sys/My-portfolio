Taking driver onboarding off paper

Paper registration was losing drivers before they ever took a shipment. I led a self-service onboarding flow that took the paper out and kept a human in the loop for the drivers who needed one.

Context

Movam builds multi-platform B2B logistics products serving 10,000+ users. Every product decision has to work for three groups at once: business customers who ship goods, driver partners who move them, and the internal operations team that keeps both running.

The problem

For the business, shipments need drivers. Every driver lost during registration was capacity we could not sell.

For drivers, joining meant gathering a stack of paper documents and handing them over, then waiting through a long, manual process.

For operations, it meant filing and finding those papers by hand. Documents went missing before we needed them. Some registrations never completed at all.

Why it mattered

The bottleneck to growth was onboarding friction, not demand.

More drivers on the platform meant more shipments and more sales. That made onboarding the shortest path from product work to revenue, and the right first priority ahead of the other capabilities on the roadmap.

My role and team

I owned the problem definition, the requirements, the backlog and the release plan. I coordinated design, engineering, operations and testing. Designers designed and engineers built; my job was to decide what we built, why, and in what order, and to keep everyone pointed at the same outcome.

Discovery

I used workflow reviews to trace how a driver got from first contact to approved and active: where documents came in, where they were stored, where they went missing and where drivers dropped out. This is the same business-process review I used across Movam to find operational bottlenecks for internal teams. Onboarding was the costliest one.

Key insights

  1. Friction, not demand, was capping growth. Paper and a long process were losing us drivers we had already won.
  2. Not every driver is comfortable with technology. Most drivers are educated, but some are not at ease with apps. A flow that only works for confident users would exclude exactly the people we were trying to recruit.

Options and trade-offs

The obvious option was full automation: verification APIs from end to end, no human in the loop. I rejected it.

OptionUpsideRiskDecision
Fully automated verificationFastest for confident users; least work for operationsDrivers who got stuck in verification had no way forward, so we would lose them againRejected
Hybrid: digital self-registration plus manual approval where neededRemoves paper for everyone; nobody is blocked by verificationKeeps some manual work for operationsChosen

I accepted some manual effort for operations in exchange for not losing drivers at the verification step. Supply mattered more than a fully automated pipeline.

What we built

A self-service partner onboarding flow. Drivers register on their phone and upload their documents in the flow. The documents go straight into our admin portal and are stored there, not on paper. Operations reviews and approves manually where a step needs judgment.

Once driver supply was moving, the roadmap moved on to alternative-route planning, geofencing and remote locking in later releases.

How I worked with design, engineering and ops

Operations were users of this product as much as drivers were. The admin side, where documents land and approvals happen, got the same attention in requirements as the driver flow.

I wrote the requirements, user stories and acceptance criteria, ran backlog prioritisation with engineering and design, and worked with testing on what "done" meant for each release. Later at Movam I added AI-assisted workflows to this work: research synthesis, requirements drafting, edge-case analysis and test planning, with every output reviewed by me and the team before it entered the backlog.

Launch and measurement

After launch, registration became much shorter and more drivers came on board. More drivers meant more shipments and more sales.

At product level, I put data-led experimentation and product feedback loops in place. They were associated with a 12% improvement in user activation.

What I learned and what I'd do next

Inclusion can be a growth lever. Keeping a human step for less confident drivers was not a compromise. It protected supply.

I would bring AI in much earlier. A lot of this work was manual and shipping took a long time. Today I would use AI-assisted workflows from the start for requirements, edge cases, test planning and document checks to shorten delivery cycles, and keep human approval wherever judgment matters.
